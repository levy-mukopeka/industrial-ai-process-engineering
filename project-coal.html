01

ENGINEERING PROBLEM

Laboratory quality is accurate.
Decision-making also needs timing.

Coal preparation decisions are made while the process is operating, while laboratory quality measurements require sampling, preparation and analysis. The resulting time gap can make it difficult to see emerging product-quality risk early enough.

The project investigated whether routinely available process variables could be used to estimate product ash continuously, then combine that prediction with uncertainty analysis and scenario testing.

FEATURED SYSTEM

CHPP Industrial AI Decision Support System

The featured prototype connects process operating conditions to product-quality prediction, uncertainty analysis and operator-facing interpretation. The interface was designed around a simple engineering chain: understand the current process state, estimate likely product quality, quantify risk, investigate the drivers and identify what deserves attention.

Input layer — feed ash, medium density, cyclone pressure and feed rate.
Prediction layer — product ash soft-sensor models for peas and nuts.
Risk layer — Monte Carlo propagation using 10,000 simulations.
Decision layer — current-state status, quality interpretation and what-if analysis.

MACHINE LEARNING

Model comparison before model selection.

Multiple algorithms were investigated so the modelling stage could be treated as an engineering model-selection problem rather than assuming one algorithm was automatically appropriate.

ModelRole in developmentEngineering interpretation
Multiple Linear RegressionBaseline relationshipEstablishes a simple, interpretable reference between process inputs and product quality.
Polynomial RegressionNonlinear functional formTests whether curvature improves on the linear baseline.
Decision TreeNon-linear rule structureUseful for investigating threshold-like relationships and interpretable splits.
Random ForestEnsemble predictionCaptures non-linear interactions with an ensemble of decision trees.
Gradient BoostingSequential ensembleBuilds predictive performance by correcting previous model errors.
XGBoostBoosted prediction layerUsed as an advanced boosting model within the industrial-AI prototype.
Model-development note

Model performance is treated as dataset- and validation-dependent. The portfolio will report measured development and validation results from the underlying project rather than presenting a model as universally accurate.

ENGINEERING INTERPRETATION

The Monte Carlo layer expands the analysis from a single predicted ash value to a range-based view of uncertainty. For Nuts Ash, the reported simulation results place the mean at 20.75%, with a 95% prediction interval extending from 17.996% to 23.527%. The 22% specification threshold falls inside that interval, and 2,121 of 10,000 reported runs are above specification.

Peas Ash shows a mean of 20.107% with a reported range of 16.549%–23.663%.

ENGINEERING ATTENTION
Prototype boundary

The current system is an industrial-AI prototype for analysis and decision support. It does not directly manipulate plant equipment and is not presented as autonomous process control. A future implementation could connect validated models to live plant data through appropriate SCADA/PLC or historian interfaces, subject to engineering validation, cybersecurity and operational governance.
